How to read our label

1

The tier is the bloodline

Black, Gold or White tells you how much pure wagyu the animal carries. More wagyu in the bloodline means more potential for marbling.

2

The score is the marbling

Every animal's beef is graded on Australia's official marble score, from MS0 to MS9+. The higher the number, the more fine, buttery fat runs through the meat.

Why it matters

Genetics set the ceiling. Marbling is what reaches your plate.

Genetics

Wagyu cattle store fat inside the muscle rather than around it, and that trait is passed down. The more wagyu in an animal's bloodline, the more marbling it can build, and the finer that marbling becomes.

That's why we name the bloodline on every label. A 100% wagyu animal and a 50% cross can both be sold as "wagyu", but they are not the same beef.

Marbling

Marbling is the fine web of fat running through the meat. As a steak cooks, the marbling melts and bastes the meat from the inside. That's where wagyu gets its tenderness, juiciness and rich, buttery flavor.

Genetics set the limit. Time on grain, 450 days or more for every animal we sell, lets the marbling develop. The score on the label tells you how it turned out.

Why do Gold and Black share scores?

Every animal is graded on its own, so an exceptional Gold can reach MS9+. The difference is the bloodline. Black is 100% wagyu, and at the same score, that pure bloodline gives finer marbling and a deeper, more buttery flavor.

*American (USDA) and Australian grades are different systems, and there's no official conversion between them. The USDA markers above are approximate, based on published industry comparisons.
