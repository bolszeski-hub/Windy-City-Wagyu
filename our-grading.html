How to read our label

1

The tier is the bloodline

Black, Gold or White tells you how much pure wagyu genetics the animal carries, and that sets how far its marbling can go.

2

The score is the marbling

Each carcass is graded on Australia's official AUS-MEAT marble score, from MS0 to MS9+. The higher the number, the more fine, buttery fat runs through the meat.

Why it matters

Genetics set the ceiling. Marbling is what reaches your plate.

Genetics

Wagyu cattle are bred to store fat inside the muscle itself rather than around it. That trait is inherited. The more wagyu in the bloodline, the more marbling the animal can build, and the finer and more delicate that marbling becomes.

That is why we name the bloodline on every label. A 100% fullblood animal and a 50% cross can both be called "wagyu", but they are not the same beef.

Marbling

Marbling is the fine web of fat running through the meat. As the steak cooks, it melts and bastes every fiber from within, and that is where wagyu gets its tenderness, juiciness and rich, buttery flavor.

Genetics decide how much marbling is possible. Time on grain, 450 days or more for every animal we sell, lets it fully develop. The grade on the label tells you how it turned out.

Why do Gold and Black share scores?

Every animal is graded on its own, so an exceptional Gold can reach MS9+. What separates the two is the bloodline. Black is 100% fullblood wagyu, and at the same score, that pure genetics brings a finer marbling texture and a deeper, more buttery flavor.

*USDA and AUS-MEAT are different grading systems, and there is no official conversion between them. The USDA markers above are approximate, based on published industry comparisons.
