01 · The breed

What makes wagyu, wagyu

Wagyu simply means "Japanese cattle". The name covers four native Japanese breeds: Japanese Black, Japanese Brown, Japanese Shorthorn and Japanese Polled. Japanese Black makes up about 95% of all wagyu, and it's the breed behind the most marbled beef in the world.

What sets wagyu apart is in its genes. These cattle store fat inside the muscle, as a fine web of marbling, rather than around it. That marbling melts as the beef cooks, giving wagyu its tenderness, juiciness and rich, buttery flavor.

02 · The history

How wagyu came to Australia

Japan guards its wagyu closely. A small number of cattle and genetics left the country before exports were banned, and Australian breeders have spent more than 30 years building on them.

  1. 1989

    The Australian Wagyu Breeders Association is founded with seven members.

  2. 1990–91

    The first wagyu cattle and genetics reach Australia, by way of the United States.

  3. 1997

    Japan declares wagyu a national treasure and bans the export of wagyu cattle and genetics.

  4. 2021

    Australia approves a grading camera, developed with Japan's own meat graders, that can score wagyu marbling right to the top of the scale.

  5. Today

    Australia has the largest herd of 100% wagyu outside Japan.

The bloodlines behind the herd

In Japan, wagyu was bred for generations within separate regions, and each region developed its own bloodline. Australia's 100% wagyu herd still carries those lines today. The largest share comes from the Tajima line of Hyogo Prefecture, the same line behind Japan's famous Kobe beef.

Compared with Japanese A5

Japanese A5 is extraordinary, and so rich that a few ounces makes a full serving. Australian wagyu shares the same bloodlines, with a little more beefy flavor. Our Black tier reaches the top of the marbling scale, while Gold and White give you wagyu you can enjoy as a full steak.

Compared with American wagyu

Most American wagyu is crossbred, and a US label doesn't have to say how much wagyu is in it. Australian producers also typically keep cattle on grain for longer. We show the bloodline and the score on every cut, so you always know what you are paying for.

04 · Grading and feeding

Australia has its own scale, and every animal is graded on it

Australian wagyu isn't scored on Japan's BMS scale. It's graded on Australia's official AUS-MEAT marble score, from MS0 to MS9+, by a qualified grader.

Since 2021, a grading camera developed with the Japanese Meat Grading Association has also been approved to score Australian wagyu across the full scale. Its measurements are based on more than 30,000 graded animals.

The results show why genetics matter: 100% wagyu typically grades around MS8, while half-wagyu, half-Angus crosses average MS6 or better.

05 · Traceability

Every animal, tracked from birth

Australia's National Livestock Identification System (NLIS) tracks every animal for its whole life. It works in three parts.

  1. 01

    Tagged at birth

    Each animal gets an official NLIS ear tag on the farm where it's born.

  2. 02

    Every property identified

    Every farm, saleyard and feedlot has its own registered ID code.

  3. 03

    Every move recorded

    A national database logs where each animal is, and every time it moves.

06 · Glossary

Speak wagyu

Fullblood
100% wagyu, with Japanese lineage on both sides and no crossbreeding. The bloodline can be confirmed by DNA. This is our Black tier.
Purebred
At least 93.75% wagyu, reached by breeding with 100% wagyu cattle over four generations. This is our Gold tier.
F1 (crossbred)
The first cross: about 50% wagyu, usually crossed with Angus. This is our White tier.
Marble score (MS)
Australia's official marbling scale, set by AUS-MEAT, from MS0 to MS9+. It's the score on every Windy City Wagyu label.
BMS
Japan's Beef Marbling Standard, from 1 to 12. It's a different scale from Australia's, and there's no official conversion between the two.
A5
Japan's top grade. "A" is the best grade for yield, meaning how much meat the animal gives, and "5" is the best grade for quality.
Kobe
A protected name for wagyu raised in Japan's Hyogo Prefecture. It describes where the beef comes from, not a breed or a grade.
NLIS
Australia's National Livestock Identification System, which tracks every animal from birth.
HGP
Hormone growth promotant, a hormone used to make cattle grow faster. Our cattle are raised without them.
Days on grain
How long cattle eat a grain diet at the end of their lives. More time on grain gives the marbling longer to develop.

07 · Questions

What people ask us

Is Australian wagyu real wagyu?

Yes. It comes from the same Japanese cattle. Our Black tier is 100% wagyu, with its Japanese lineage confirmed by Australian Wagyu Association pedigree and DNA records.

Is this Kobe beef?

No. Kobe is a protected name for wagyu raised in Hyogo Prefecture, Japan. Much of Australia's 100% wagyu herd descends from Hyogo's Tajima bloodline, but we will never call our beef Kobe, and no wagyu raised outside Hyogo should carry that name.

How does MS9+ compare with Japanese A5?

The two use different scales, so there's no exact conversion. MS9+ is the very top of Australia's scale, and industry guides commonly place it near the top of Japan's scale, the level of marbling found in A5.

Why not just buy American wagyu?

There's excellent American beef. But most American wagyu is crossbred, and a US label doesn't have to say how much wagyu is in it. With us, the bloodline and the marble score are always on the label.

Why is the same tier priced differently?

Every animal is graded on its own. A Gold MS9+ has more marbling than a Gold MS6, and it's priced to match. The tier tells you the bloodline; the score tells you how that animal graded.

Does imported mean frozen?

Our wagyu arrives chilled and vacuum-sealed [never frozen: to confirm with producers].
